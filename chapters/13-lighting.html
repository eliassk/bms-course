Chapter 13

Lighting control

Lighting is the system people notice first and forgive last. This chapter covers the ideas every lighting control system shares, then DALI in depth, the platforms built on it, DMX for stage work, sensors that save energy, emergency lighting that must be tested, and colour temperature control.

13.1 Lighting control concepts: groups, scenes, fades

Almost every lighting control system, from a two-room DALI install to a stadium, is built from the same four ideas. Learn them once and every product's configuration tool starts to look familiar.

ConceptWhat it isTypical example
Channel / addressThe smallest thing you can control on its own. One driver, one DALI short address, one DMX slot.Downlight driver A12
GroupA named set of channels that receive one command together."Window row", "All corridor"
SceneA stored snapshot: a level for each channel. Recalling it moves every member to its own level."Presentation": front 20 %, rest 60 %
FadeHow long the change takes. Applies to scene recalls and level changes.3 s to scene, 60 s to off

Why scenes beat direct levels

Sending "go to scene 3" is one message. Sending twelve individual levels is twelve messages, and they arrive at slightly different times, so the room visibly ripples. On DALI, scene levels are stored inside each driver. A single broadcast "go to scene 3" makes every driver look up its own value and fade there in sync. The controller does not even need to know the levels.

A channel can sit in several groups at once. A window-row downlight might be in "Window row", "Zone A" and "All lights". Groups overlap; scenes do not need to cover every channel. On DALI a channel can also be told to ignore a scene (stored value 255, "MASK"), so it stays where it is when that scene is recalled.

Fades and the human eye

Fast changes feel abrupt. Slow fades feel calm and hide the moment of switching. Rules of thumb used by lighting designers:

  • 0–1 s for a manual button press. People expect a response.
  • 2–4 s for scene changes in meeting rooms and restaurants.
  • 30–90 s for automatic changes the occupant did not ask for, such as daylight dimming or a presence sensor dimming down after a timeout.
  • Very long fades (minutes) for theatre house lights and circadian transitions.

Try it: groups, scenes and fades

A typical conference room has a wall keypad with four buttons: "Full", "Meeting", "Presentation" and "Off". The AV system can recall the same "Presentation" scene when the projector turns on. The keypad and the AV processor send the same scene number. Neither needs to know the individual levels.
Scene numbering drift is a classic commissioning bug. If one floor uses scene 3 for "Presentation" and another uses scene 3 for "Cleaning", a building-wide recall from the BMS does strange things. Agree a scene table for the whole building before programming starts.

Why does a DALI scene recall look smoother than sending each luminaire its level one by one?

  • Channel, group, scene, fade: four ideas behind every lighting system.
  • Scenes are snapshots stored close to the light; one command recalls many levels.
  • Fade time is a design choice. Automatic changes should be slow.

13.2 DALI system design: addressing, PSUs, limits

DALI (Digital Addressable Lighting Interface) is the international standard IEC 62386 for digital lighting control. DALI-2 is the current generation, with certified interoperability and standard parts for input devices (buttons, sensors). The DALI Alliance runs the certification programme.

The bus

  • Two wires, polarity-free at the devices. Any topology: line, star, tree. No termination resistors.
  • The bus is idle at about 16 V (allowed roughly 9.5–22.5 V). A device signals by shorting it low.
  • Speed: 1200 bit/s, Manchester (bi-phase) coded. A forward frame from the controller is 16 bits (address byte + command byte), with 24-bit frames for input devices. A reply ("backward frame") is 8 bits.
  • One bus power supply per line, limited to 250 mA.
  • Voltage drop on the cable MUST stay under 2 V. In practice this gives about 300 m with 1.5 mm² cable.
  • The DALI pair may run in the same cable as mains (it is usually treated as basic-insulated, not SELV), which saves cabling. Check local wiring rules.

Addresses and limits per line

ItemLimit per DALI lineNote
Control gear (drivers)64 short addresses (0–63)Many drivers can share one address, but then you cannot tell them apart.
Control devices (DALI-2 inputs)64 more addressesButtons, presence and light sensors; separate address space.
Groups16 (0–15)Membership stored in each driver.
Scenes16 (0–15)Level per scene stored in each driver.
Bus current250 mAEach device draws about 2 mA (up to ~6 mA for some input devices).

Addresses are assigned during commissioning. The controller tells all unaddressed drivers to pick a random 24-bit number, then runs a binary search ("compare, withdraw") to find them one at a time and give each a short address. This is why a fresh line takes a minute or two to address and why you then have to identify which address is which physical light (flash it and note the location).

The logarithmic dimming curve

DALI levels ("arc power") run from 0 to 254. Level 0 is off. Level 255 is reserved for "MASK". The standard curve is logarithmic: level 1 is 0.1 % light, level 254 is 100 %, and every 3 % step in level is roughly the same perceived step. The formula is:

light % = 10 ^ ((level − 1) × 3 / 253 − 1)

The eye responds to light roughly logarithmically, so equal level steps look like equal brightness steps. Halving the level does not halve the light: level 127 is only about 3 %. A linear curve (also selectable on many DT6 LED drivers) gives far too little control at the bottom end.

Device types

IEC 62386 parts 2xx define extra features per device type (DT). The most common:

DTPartDevice
DT1202Self-contained emergency lighting (test commands, battery status)
DT6207LED driver
DT8209Colour control: tunable white (Tc), RGBWAF, xy

Try it: size a DALI line

An open-plan office floor of 1,200 m² might have 180 LED panels. Designers typically split that into four lines of about 45 drivers each, leaving room for later additions and for a few DALI-2 sensors per line, each line with its own 250 mA supply.
Plan for no more than about 50 drivers per line in a new design. Hitting exactly 64 leaves no spare addresses for a replacement driver, a later tenant change or an extra sensor. Also do not add a second bus PSU to the same line unless both supplies are designed for it: currents add and can exceed the 250 mA the standard allows.

A DALI line has 58 drivers on one PSU and 380 m of 1.5 mm² cable to the farthest driver. What is the most likely problem?

  • One line: 64 drivers, 64 input devices, 16 groups, 16 scenes, 250 mA, 2 V drop, ~300 m.
  • 1200 bit/s: DALI is for commands, not for streaming effects.
  • The logarithmic curve maps 254 levels to 0.1–100 % light in equal perceived steps.

13.3 Helvar and other DALI platforms

A single DALI line is a small, slow island. Real buildings have dozens of lines. A lighting control platform ties them together: routers or controllers with two or more DALI outputs, an Ethernet backbone between them, configuration software, and an integration interface for the BMS.

Common architecture

Helvar as a worked example

Helvar (Finland) is a common example of this architecture. Its DALI routers (for example the 9xx series) each drive two DALI lines and connect over Ethernet. Programming is done in the Designer software. Routers are addressed with a four-part address of the form cluster.router.subnet.device, so 1.2.1.15 means cluster 1, router 2, DALI line 1, device 15. Logic is organised around groups and scenes that span routers: the routers exchange group commands over the network so one button press can move lights on several lines.

For integration, Helvar documents a TCP/UDP text protocol called HelvarNet (commands such as "recall scene", "direct level", "query load status"). Gateways from several vendors, and open-source integrations, use it. Treat exact command syntax and port numbers as something to check against the current vendor documentation for the firmware in front of you.

Other platforms

What to ask of any platform

Is the integration protocol documented and open?Avoids a single integrator lock-in.
Can it report lamp and driver failures per address?Maintenance needs to know which fitting failed.
Can I export the project file?Without it, nobody else can change the system.

A typical integration: the BMS reads "lamp failure" and "power" per area every few minutes and recalls scene 1 ("Building closed") at night. Everything that must react within a second (buttons, presence) stays in the lighting routers.
Do not let the BMS poll every DALI address quickly. At 1200 bit/s a query-and-reply takes roughly 20–40 ms, so polling 64 devices can occupy the line for seconds and make buttons feel slow. Poll slowly, prefer event reports, and query per area rather than per driver where the platform allows.

What is the main job of a DALI router in a multi-line system?

  • Platforms = DALI lines + routers + IP backbone + software + integration API.
  • Keep fast logic local; the BMS sends areas and scenes, reads faults and energy.
  • Ask for open protocols, local fallback and exportable project files.

13.4 DMX for stage and events

Where DALI is slow and smart, DMX512 is fast and dumb. Defined by ANSI E1.11, it streams the level of every channel, all the time, from a lighting console to the fixtures. Moving heads, LED wash lights, hazers and dimmer racks all speak it.

The signal

  • Physical layer: RS-485 differential pair, 5-pin XLR (3-pin is common but non-standard), daisy chained.
  • One universe = up to 512 slots (channels), each 8 bits (0–255).
  • 250 kbit/s. Each slot is 11 bits (start, 8 data, 2 stop) = 44 µs.
  • A packet starts with a BREAK (at least 88 µs low), a mark-after-break, then a start code (0 for dimmer data), then the slots.
  • A full 512-slot universe refreshes about 44 times per second.
  • Up to 32 unit loads per segment; the last device MUST be terminated with 120 Ω.

There is no addressing in the packet. Each fixture is set (by DIP switches or a menu) to a start address and simply reads its block of consecutive slots. A moving head that uses 16 channels, set to address 101, reads slots 101–116. Fine control uses two slots per parameter (coarse + fine), giving 16-bit resolution for pan, tilt or a smooth dimmer.

In a typical theatre the console sends 8–20 universes of sACN over a dedicated lighting network. Nodes in each lighting bar convert universes back to DMX for the fixtures. House lights are often DALI for daily use, with a DMX-to-DALI interface or a dimmer system that also listens to DMX so the console can fade them during a show.
DMX has no error checking and no acknowledgement. Fixtures keep the last values they received when the signal is lost, or go dark after a timeout, depending on their settings. Decide "hold last look" vs "black out" per venue, and never use DMX alone for anything safety related, such as house lights in an emergency. Those MUST be forced on by an independent path.

A fixture uses 12 channels and is set to start address 505. What happens?

  • DMX512: 512 slots × 8 bits, 250 kbit/s, ~44 Hz refresh, no addressing, no error checking.
  • Fixtures read a block from a start address; avoid overlaps and the 512 edge.
  • Art-Net and sACN carry many universes over IP; RDM adds discovery and diagnostics.

13.5 Presence and daylight sensors

Lighting in an office runs for thousands of hours a year. The cheapest saving is to turn it off when nobody is there and to dim it when the sun does the work. Studies and standards (for example the LENI method in EN 15193-1) credit occupancy and daylight control with savings in the range of 20–60 %, depending heavily on the room and how it is used.

Sensing technologies

TechnologySensesStrengthWeakness
Passive infrared (PIR)Moving heat sources crossing zonesCheap, low power, no false triggers through wallsMisses still people typing; needs line of sight
Microwave / radarDoppler movementVery sensitive, small movementsCan see through thin walls and glass; false triggers
UltrasonicDoppler in airFills the room, around partitionsAir movement can trigger it
Dual techPIR + ultrasonic or microwaveSwitch on with both, hold with eitherCost
Light sensor (photocell)Illuminance on the area belowEnables daylight dimmingReads reflected light, needs calibration

Presence vs absence detection

Presence (auto-on, auto-off): lights switch on when someone enters and off after the hold time. Good for corridors and toilets. Absence (manual-on, auto-off): people press a button to switch on; the sensor only switches off. In daylit rooms, absence detection usually saves more, because people often do not switch on at all when it is bright enough.

The hold time (time delay) is the key parameter. Too short and lights go off on people sitting still. Too long and savings drop. 10–20 minutes is common in offices, with a dim-to-low stage (for example 10 % for 5 minutes) before off in corridors, so nobody is left in the dark.

Daylight harvesting

A ceiling light sensor measures the light reflected from the desk area. A closed-loop controller dims the luminaires until the total (daylight + artificial) matches the target, for example 500 lx on the work plane, the EN 12464-1 value for office desk work. Rows near the window get more daylight, so they are usually a separate group with their own sensor or an offset.

Try it: a day in a daylit office

A typical open-plan office: dual-tech sensors in a grid every 6–8 m, each controlling its own DALI group. The BMS reads each sensor's occupancy, so the same signal can also reduce ventilation and heating setpoints in empty zones. One sensor, three systems.
Daylight sensors MUST be calibrated in the finished room, with furniture in place. A sensor set up over a bare grey floor reads quite differently once a white desk sits under it. Also keep the sensor's view away from the window itself, or direct sunlight will make it dim the room far too much.

People in a meeting room complain the lights go off during long video calls. What is the first thing to check?

  • PIR for movement, microwave/ultrasonic for small movement, dual-tech for reliability.
  • Absence detection plus daylight dimming usually saves most in daylit rooms.
  • Hold time and calibration decide whether people love or hate the system.

13.6 Emergency lighting and testing

Emergency lighting lets people leave safely when normal power fails. It is a life-safety system. It is designed to standards (in Europe, EN 1838 for light levels and EN 50172 for system and testing; in the UK, BS 5266-1; in the US, NFPA 101), and it MUST keep working with no BMS, no network and no mains.

Types and terms

TermMeaning
Escape route lightingLights the route. EN 1838: at least 1 lx on the centre line of routes up to 2 m wide.
Open area (anti-panic)At least 0.5 lx on the floor of larger open areas.
High-risk task areaAt least 10 % of normal lighting (and not less than 15 lx) so a dangerous process can be shut down.
MaintainedThe emergency lamp is on all the time (e.g. exit signs in theatres).
Non-maintainedOn only when mains fails.
Self-containedBattery in each luminaire. Most common in small and mid-size buildings.
Central batteryOne battery system feeds many luminaires over fire-resistant cable.
Rated durationUsually 1 h or 3 h. 3 h is common where people may stay after the event, or re-occupy at once.

Testing

Batteries and lamps fail silently. Standards therefore require regular tests and a logbook:

  • Daily (where required): visual check of central system indicators.
  • Monthly: a short function test. Simulate mains failure and check every luminaire lights. Long enough to prove operation, short enough not to drain the battery much.
  • Annually: a full duration test. Run on battery for the whole rated time (1 h or 3 h) and check the luminaires are still lit at the end.

After a full duration test the batteries need up to about 24 hours to recharge. During that time the building is under-protected. The usual fix is to test only half the luminaires at a time, alternating neighbours, or to test when the building is empty and stays empty for the next day.

Automatic test systems do this for you. DALI DT1 (IEC 62386-202) emergency drivers accept "start function test" and "start duration test" commands, run them, and store the result: lamp failure, battery failure, duration test failed, and the time the battery lasted. The controller collects the results for the logbook.

Try it: a year of tests

A typical office building with 300 self-contained DALI emergency luminaires runs monthly function tests automatically in the early hours, and the annual duration test on two weekends: odd addresses one weekend, even addresses the next. The BMS shows a red tile per failed luminaire and exports the log as a PDF for the fire inspector.
A BMS view of test results is helpful, but the legal record is the logbook the responsible person keeps. Also remember that a failed duration test usually means a battery replacement: batteries in self-contained fittings commonly last around 4 years, so plan budget for it.

Why are annual duration tests often staggered between neighbouring luminaires?

  • Emergency lighting is life safety: independent of BMS and mains.
  • Monthly function test, annual full-duration test, logbook.
  • Plan for the recharge window: stagger tests or test when the building stays empty.

13.7 Tunable white and circadian lighting

White LED light comes in different correlated colour temperatures (CCT), measured in kelvin. Low values are warm and yellow, high values cool and blue. A tunable white luminaire has two LED channels, warm (say 2700 K) and cool (say 6500 K), and mixes them to land anywhere between.

CCTLooks likeTypical use
1800–2200 KCandle, sunsetBars, evening hospitality
2700–3000 KWarm white, incandescentHomes, hotels, restaurants
4000 KNeutral whiteOffices, schools, retail
5000–6500 KCool white, overcast daylightInspection, healthcare, some industry

How it is controlled

  • DALI DT8 Tc: one address takes a level and a colour temperature. DT8 expresses colour temperature in mirek (1,000,000 / K), so 4000 K = 250 mirek. Mirek is used because equal mirek steps look roughly equally different.
  • Two DALI DT6 addresses: one for warm, one for cool. The controller does the mixing. Simple, but scenes need two values and fades can drift in colour.
  • DMX: usually intensity + CCT channels, or raw warm/cool channels.

When mixing two channels, total output is not constant: at the midpoint both run at part output, and depending on the driver the luminaire may be brighter or dimmer in the middle of the range. Good DT8 drivers compensate so brightness stays the same while CCT moves.

Try it: mix warm and cool

Circadian lighting

The eye has light-sensitive cells (ipRGCs, containing melanopsin) that are most sensitive to blue-cyan light around 480–490 nm. They influence the body clock and alertness. The CIE S 026 standard defines melanopic EDI (equivalent daylight illuminance) as a measure of this effect. Guidance published in 2022 by a group of researchers (Brown et al.) recommends at least about 250 lx melanopic EDI at the eye during the day and much less in the evening. The WELL building standard has related criteria.

A circadian schedule therefore runs higher intensity and cooler CCT in the morning and midday, and warmer, dimmer light in the evening. Note that intensity at the eye matters at least as much as CCT: a bright 3000 K room can have more melanopic effect than a dim 6500 K one. The science is active and claims about health benefits should be treated with care.

In a typical care home the corridor and day-room luminaires run 5000 K at 600 lx in the late morning and fall gradually to 2700 K at 150 lx after 19:00. Night-time paths use very low, warm light so residents can find the toilet without being fully woken.
Check what the luminaire does at low levels. Many tunable white drivers cannot hold an accurate CCT below a few per cent output, and cheap two-channel mixing can shift visibly green or pink (a large Duv) in the middle of the range. Ask for colour consistency data (MacAdam steps) over the full range.

What is 5000 K expressed in mirek?

  • CCT in kelvin; DALI DT8 uses mirek = 1,000,000 / K.
  • Tunable white mixes warm and cool channels; good drivers keep brightness and colour consistent.
  • Circadian effect depends on intensity at the eye as much as on CCT.
